  Two extracts of the Protomaps daily planet build
  https://build.protomaps.com/20260819.pmtiles  (z0-15, ~120 GB)
  merged into one archive:

    pmtiles extract <planet> world-z0-5.pmtiles --maxzoom=5
    pmtiles extract <planet> us-z6-8.pmtiles \
        --bbox=-98.5,28.5,-73.0,43.0 --minzoom=6 --maxzoom=8
    pmtiles merge world-z0-5.pmtiles us-z6-8.pmtiles \
        protomaps-basemap.pmtiles
    pmtiles verify protomaps-basemap.pmtiles

  go-pmtiles 1.31.2. Both halves are needed:

  - The world at z0-5 (15 MB) because all four maps open by fitting their
    markers, and the widest marker set spans Texas to Massachusetts -- about
    23 degrees of longitude, which Leaflet fits at roughly z4 in a desktop
    viewport. A US-only extract leaves the surrounding ocean and the rest of
    the continent blank at exactly the zoom the maps open at.

  - The eastern and central United States at z6-8 (19 MB) because that is
    where every marker is. All 48 mapping markers on this site fall inside
    lat 29.30-42.33, lng -97.52 to -74.01: vaudeville and African-American
    theatre circuits, and the towns Victor and Columbia sent scouts to. The
    bbox above is that envelope with about a degree of padding.

  z8 is where county outlines, the interstate network and town names resolve
  -- enough to say which city a theatre was in, which is the whole claim these
  maps make. Buying more depth costs a lot over an area this wide: the same
  bbox at z0-9 is 71 MB and at z0-10 is 214 MB, because tile count quadruples
  per level. The renderer over-zooms past z8, so panning and zooming still
  work; the geometry is just not re-cut. modules/Mapping/asset/js/omeka-basemap.js
  caps the map controls at z10, two levels past the data.

  KNOWN BUG: `pmtiles extract` with --bbox crashes with
  `fatal error: concurrent map writes` -- a data race in its HTTP range reader
  -- roughly half the time. It fails loudly and leaves a headerless output
  file, so it cannot corrupt a good archive silently: retry until
  `pmtiles verify` passes.

theatre circuits and recording expeditions between 1900 and 1926, and OSM's
coverage of those places does not change in ways that matter here.
