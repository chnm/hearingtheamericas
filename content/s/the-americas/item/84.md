  - text: Dan Quinn had a long stage career. This song, written in 1902, has been recorded many times. The cover image depicts the rascally Bailey and his wife, dressed as a stock “Mammy” character. Bailey is a flashy character in the mold of "Zip Coon," a stock minstrel figure. Quinn sings in imitation of what he takes to be African American speech and describes Mrs. Bailey “bellerin' like a prune-fed calf.” Notice especially how he exaggerates the pitch bends, for example on “backYARD” and “weepin’ HARD starting at 25 seconds. Long, swooping pitch bends were part of the minstrel show’s parody of African American singing styles. As was common in the minstrel show, the song plays her sorrow for laughs. The song has a slight “ragtime” feel.
